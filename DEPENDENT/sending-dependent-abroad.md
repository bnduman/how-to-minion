# Sending DEPENDENT to International Addressees

> **The short version:** Anything leaving the country has to clear customs. The university's customs office (Zollstelle) does that for us. We send them **one** application form plus **one** Excel sheet with all the addresses. They send back a proforma invoice for every parcel and a one-page export approval.

---

## Step 1: Fill Out the Export Application

Open **Exportabwicklung_Antrag_BCDSS.xlsx**. It is bilingual (German / English) and split into numbered sections. Most of it stays the same every time. Check these and update whatever changed:

**Section 1 — Applicant's data**
Institute name and address stay as they are. The name, phone number, and email fields are deliberately left blank — fill in **your own**, since you are the one the customs office will call if something is unclear.

**Section 2 — Consignment's data**
- **Description of goods** — the magazine and its link on our website. Already filled in.
- **Total value of goods in euros** — we declare **1 €**. DEPENDENT is free.
- **Quantity of packages** — how many parcels are going out this time.
- **Net weight of goods** — the total weight.

**Section 3 — Pick up location**
Where the parcels will be waiting for collection.

**Section 5 — End use**
- **Type of dispatch** — pick **Gift parcel** from the dropdown.
- **What are the goods used for?** — choose *sonstiges / other* from the dropdown, then write the actual end use one line below. The form is strict about this: it wants something concrete, not "research". Last time we wrote **"Magazine to read and look at"**, and that went through fine.
- **Cyber-surveillance items?** — *nein / no*.

> 💡 Leave **section 4 (Empfänger / consignee)** blank. The recipients come from the address sheet instead. **Section 6** is for the customs office to fill in, not you. The tariff code and gross weight fields were left empty last time and nobody asked for them.

---

## Step 2: Prepare the Address Sheet

Open **Exportabwicklung_Antrag_BCDSS_Adressen.xlsx**. It contains the header row and two example rows showing the format. Delete the examples and enter your own. The format is deliberately simple:

1. One addressee per row.
2. The whole address goes in a **single cell**, written out over several lines exactly as it should appear on the envelope: name and title, institution and department, street, postcode and city, and the country on the last line.
3. Everyone goes in the same sheet. Do not split them into one file per person.

> 💡 Pick one language for the country names and stick to it. Mixing *Großbritannien* and *United Kingdom* in the same list still gets through, but it reads sloppily.

---

## Step 3: Send Everything to the Customs Office

One email to **[Zoll@verwaltung.uni-bonn.de](mailto:Zoll@verwaltung.uni-bonn.de)** with both files attached. That is the whole submission.

---

## Step 4: What Comes Back, and What to Do With It

The customs office replies with two different things. They are not treated the same way.

### Proforma invoices — one per address

You get a separate invoice for every addressee. Print them and attach each one to the **outside** of the matching envelope, in its own separate envelope.

> ⚠️ Match them carefully. Each invoice belongs to one specific parcel, and an invoice on the wrong envelope means the wrong parcel gets held up.

### Export approval — one page, once

This one is not per-parcel. Print it **once** and lay it loosely on top of the pile of letters. It does not need to be attached to anything.

| Step | What | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Export application, filled out once | Email attachment |
